# Pillar 7: Lead Management & Sales Enablement
### Marketing Infrastructure Development — Implementation Blueprint

Pillar 6 focuses on bringing potential customers into the business through advertising and other acquisition channels. Pillar 7 ensures those potential customers are not ignored, poorly managed, or lost because the business has no proper sales process.

The objective is to make sure every lead has a record, a clear status, an assigned next action, and a defined path towards conversion.

This pillar is not about taking over the client's sales department. It is about designing, configuring, documenting, and improving the systems and processes that enable the business to manage leads and sell more effectively.

## 1. Lead management strategy and process assessment
Before implementing a CRM or creating follow-up processes, understand how the business currently receives and handles potential customers.

### Tasks and activities
- Identify what qualifies as a lead for the business.
- Identify all existing lead sources:
  - Website inquiries.
  - Landing page forms.
  - Social media messages and comments.
  - WhatsApp inquiries.
  - Phone calls.
  - Email inquiries.
  - Advertising lead forms.
  - Referrals.
  - Physical visits and walk-ins.
  - Marketplace inquiries.
- Understand how leads currently move from initial inquiry to purchase.
- Identify who receives inquiries and who is responsible for responding.
- Examine how the business currently records customer details and conversations.
- Determine how leads are followed up.
- Identify where leads are being lost, forgotten, delayed, or poorly handled.
- Establish the business's sales objectives and lead management requirements.
- Define response-time expectations and follow-up responsibilities.
- Identify differences in the sales process for different products, services, or customer types.
### Key deliverables
- Lead management process assessment.
- Lead source inventory.
- Existing sales process documentation.
- Lead handling gap analysis.
- Lead management requirements document.
- Recommended lead management framework.
## 2. Lead capture and centralized intake infrastructure
Every relevant inquiry should enter an organized system rather than remain scattered across different platforms and employees' personal accounts.

### Tasks and activities
- Audit all existing lead capture points.
- Identify opportunities to create additional lead capture channels.
- Configure website inquiry and contact forms.
- Configure landing page lead capture forms.
- Connect advertising platform lead forms where applicable.
- Establish procedures for capturing leads from WhatsApp, social media, calls, referrals, and physical inquiries.
- Connect supported lead sources to a central CRM or lead database.
- Ensure lead source information is captured.
- Capture campaign and advertising information where available, including UTM parameters.
- Create consistent form fields across relevant channels.
- Configure lead submission confirmations and internal notifications.
- Test whether submitted leads reach the correct destination.
- Establish procedures for manually recording leads from sources that cannot be integrated.
- Prevent duplicate records when the same person submits inquiries through multiple channels.
### Key deliverables
- Centralized lead capture infrastructure.
- Configured lead capture forms.
- Connected advertising lead sources.
- Lead source identification structure.
- Lead intake and notification procedures.
- Lead capture testing report.
## 3. CRM platform selection, setup and configuration
A Customer Relationship Management (CRM) system provides a central place to store lead information, manage sales opportunities, record interactions, and monitor conversion progress.

### Tasks and activities
- Determine whether the business needs a dedicated CRM or whether an existing platform is sufficient.
- Evaluate suitable CRM platforms based on:
  - Business size.
  - Number of leads.
  - Sales process complexity.
  - Number of users.
  - Budget.
  - Required integrations.
  - Reporting capabilities.
  - Ease of use.
- Select the most appropriate CRM solution.
- Create and configure the CRM account.
- Configure business details and relevant settings.
- Create the required lead and contact records.
- Configure sales pipelines.
- Create custom fields for business-specific information.
- Set up user accounts and roles.
- Configure access permissions.
- Establish ownership of lead records.
- Configure relevant notifications and reminders.
- Connect the CRM to approved lead sources.
- Test the CRM using sample leads.
### Key deliverables
- CRM platform recommendation.
- Configured CRM account.
- CRM user and permission structure.
- Configured lead records and custom fields.
- Configured sales pipeline.
- CRM integration requirements.
- CRM setup and testing documentation.
## 4. Lead data structure and record management
A CRM is only useful when the information stored inside it is organized, accurate, and sufficient to support sales decisions.

### Tasks and activities
- Define the information that must be collected for each lead.
- Configure appropriate lead record fields, such as:
  - Full name.
  - Phone number.
  - Email address.
  - Company name, where relevant.
  - Location.
  - Product or service of interest.
  - Inquiry details.
  - Lead source.
  - Campaign or advertisement source, where available.
  - Date received.
  - Assigned salesperson or team.
  - Lead status.
  - Qualification status.
  - Estimated deal value, where applicable.
  - Last contact date.
  - Next follow-up date.
  - Consent and communication preferences.
  - Sales notes and interaction history.
- Establish consistent naming and data entry conventions.
- Configure required fields where necessary.
- Validate email addresses and phone numbers where supported.
- Identify and merge duplicate records carefully.
- Establish procedures for updating lead information.
- Define who can create, edit, assign, and delete records.
- Establish data retention and record-cleaning procedures.
### Key deliverables
- Lead data structure and field dictionary.
- Configured CRM record templates.
- Lead data entry standards.
- Duplicate management procedures.
- Lead data quality guidelines.
- Data access and ownership rules.
## 5. Lead qualification and scoring framework
Not every inquiry represents an equally valuable sales opportunity. The business needs a consistent way to determine which leads are suitable, interested, and ready to buy.

### Tasks and activities
- Define the characteristics of a qualified lead for each product or service.
- Identify the questions that help determine whether a lead is a good fit.
- Assess relevant qualification factors:
  - Customer needs.
  - Interest in the product or service.
  - Budget or purchasing capacity, where relevant.
  - Decision-making authority, where relevant.
  - Purchase urgency.
  - Expected purchase timeline.
  - Business or personal suitability.
- Develop a qualification framework appropriate to the business.
- Use a framework such as BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) where appropriate, rather than forcing it onto every business.
- Define lead categories such as:
  - Hot leads.
  - Warm leads.
  - Cold leads.
  - Unqualified leads.
- Establish criteria for Marketing Qualified Leads (MQLs) and Sales Qualified Leads (SQLs), if the business needs these distinctions.
- Create a lead scoring model where sufficient data exists.
- Assign scores based on meaningful engagement and buying signals.
- Define what should happen when a lead meets the qualification threshold.
- Record reasons for disqualifying leads.
### Key deliverables
- Lead qualification framework.
- Lead qualification questions.
- Lead scoring criteria, where applicable.
- Hot, warm, and cold lead definitions.
- MQL and SQL criteria, where applicable.
- Lead disqualification categories.
- Configured qualification fields and scoring rules.
## 6. Lead segmentation, assignment and routing
Leads should be directed to the appropriate person or team based on the nature of their inquiry and the business's operational structure.

### Tasks and activities
- Define the categories by which leads should be segmented.
- Segment leads according to:
  - Product or service interest.
  - Geographic location.
  - Customer type.
  - Lead quality.
  - Purchase urgency.
  - Estimated value.
  - Lead source.
  - Language or specialist requirements, where relevant.
- Identify the employees or teams responsible for handling each category.
- Establish lead assignment rules.
- Configure manual or automated lead routing where supported.
- Consider round-robin assignment if leads need to be distributed evenly.
- Define procedures for high-value or urgent inquiries.
- Establish escalation procedures for unassigned or unattended leads.
- Ensure each active lead has a clearly identified owner.
- Test assignment and routing rules.
### Key deliverables
- Lead segmentation framework.
- Lead assignment and ownership structure.
- Lead routing rules.
- Escalation procedures.
- Configured CRM assignment settings.
- Lead routing test report.
## 7. Sales pipeline and opportunity management
A sales pipeline provides visibility into where each potential customer stands in the purchasing process.

### Tasks and activities
- Map the business's actual sales process.
- Identify the stages a lead passes through before becoming a customer.
- Define suitable pipeline stages, such as:
  - New lead.
  - Contact attempted.
  - Contacted.
  - Qualified.
  - Consultation or demonstration scheduled.
  - Quotation or proposal sent.
  - Negotiation.
  - Won.
  - Lost.
- Define the conditions for moving a lead from one stage to another.
- Configure the sales pipeline inside the CRM.
- Establish procedures for updating lead and opportunity statuses.
- Record estimated deal values where relevant.
- Record expected closing dates where meaningful.
- Require a next action for active opportunities.
- Record reasons for lost deals.
- Create separate pipelines where the business has genuinely different sales processes.
- Ensure leads do not remain indefinitely in inactive stages.
### Key deliverables
- Documented sales pipeline.
- Configured CRM pipeline stages.
- Pipeline stage definitions and movement criteria.
- Opportunity management procedures.
- Deal value and closing-date fields.
- Lost-deal reason categories.
## 8. Lead follow-up and response management
A lead can be interested in a product but still fail to purchase because the business responds too late, follows up inconsistently, or does not provide the information needed to make a decision.

### Tasks and activities
- Establish expected response times for incoming inquiries.
- Define who must respond to new leads.
- Establish a follow-up schedule based on lead type and urgency.
- Define the appropriate communication channels:
  - Phone calls.
  - WhatsApp.
  - Email.
  - SMS.
  - Other approved messaging channels.
- Create procedures for initial contact.
- Develop follow-up intervals and contact attempt limits.
- Configure CRM reminders and follow-up tasks.
- Create communication templates for common situations.
- Record contact attempts and conversation outcomes.
- Establish procedures for unanswered calls and messages.
- Define when a lead should be marked as unreachable or inactive.
- Establish opt-out and communication preference procedures.
- Ensure follow-up practices respect applicable privacy and marketing consent requirements.
### Key deliverables
- Lead response and follow-up policy.
- Response-time standards.
- Follow-up schedule.
- Initial contact and follow-up templates.
- Configured CRM reminders and tasks.
- Lead interaction logging procedures.
- Unresponsive lead handling process.
## 9. Sales enablement materials and process documentation
Sales enablement means equipping the people responsible for selling with the information, resources, and guidance they need to communicate value, handle objections, and convert qualified leads.

### Tasks and activities
- Identify the information salespeople regularly need when speaking with prospects.
- Develop product and service information sheets.
- Create clear product or service descriptions and benefit summaries.
- Prepare pricing sheets and package comparisons.
- Develop sales scripts for common inquiry types.
- Create call guides and discovery questions.
- Prepare responses to frequently asked questions.
- Document common customer objections and recommended responses.
- Create competitor comparison materials where reliable information is available.
- Prepare case studies, testimonials, and proof of results where available and authorized.
- Develop product demonstrations or demo guides where relevant.
- Create proposal and quotation templates.
- Document the sales process from first contact to closing.
- Establish guidelines for communicating the brand's value and differentiators.
- Organize sales materials in a shared, accessible location.
- Establish a process for reviewing and updating outdated materials.
### Key deliverables
- Sales enablement strategy.
- Sales scripts and call guides.
- Product and service information sheets.
- Pricing and package comparison sheets.
- FAQ and objection-handling documents.
- Sales presentation and demonstration materials.
- Proposal and quotation templates.
- Sales playbook.
- Centralized sales resource library.
## 10. Appointment, consultation, quotation and proposal management
For businesses that require consultations, demonstrations, site visits, or quotations before a purchase, these activities need a clear management process.

### Tasks and activities
- Determine which products or services require appointments or consultations.
- Define the booking process and the information required before an appointment.
- Configure appointment scheduling tools where appropriate.
- Connect booking tools to relevant calendars.
- Define appointment availability and scheduling rules.
- Configure booking confirmations and reminders.
- Establish procedures for rescheduling, cancellations, and missed appointments.
- Create consultation or discovery meeting templates.
- Establish how meeting outcomes and next steps are recorded in the CRM.
- Create standardized quotation and proposal templates.
- Define the information required before issuing a quotation.
- Establish quotation approval procedures where necessary.
- Define quotation validity periods and revision procedures.
- Track quotations and proposals sent to prospects.
- Establish follow-up procedures for outstanding quotations.
- Configure digital acceptance, signatures, or payment links where relevant and supported.
### Key deliverables
- Appointment and consultation workflow.
- Configured booking system, where required.
- Calendar integration.
- Appointment confirmation and reminder templates.
- Consultation documentation templates.
- Quotation and proposal templates.
- Quotation approval and follow-up procedures.
- Proposal status tracking structure.
## 11. Lead nurturing and reactivation handover
Not every lead is ready to purchase immediately. Some need more information, time, reassurance, or a future opportunity to buy.

This section establishes how such leads remain visible and are not simply abandoned.

### Tasks and activities
- Identify leads that are interested but not ready to purchase.
- Define categories for leads that require longer-term nurturing.
- Establish reasons for delayed purchasing.
- Create criteria for moving leads into a nurturing status.
- Record the customer's interests, objections, and expected purchase timeline.
- Schedule future follow-up activities.
- Identify leads that should be reactivated after a specified period.
- Establish procedures for reviewing inactive leads.
- Identify opportunities to re-engage previous prospects with relevant offers or information.
- Define when a lead should be returned to active sales follow-up.
- Establish how the sales team should hand over leads to email marketing or customer engagement activities.
- Ensure consent and communication preferences are respected before marketing messages are sent.
### Key deliverables
- Lead nurturing classification framework.
- Long-term follow-up procedures.
- Lead reactivation criteria.
- Inactive lead management process.
- Sales-to-marketing handover procedure.
- CRM nurturing and reactivation status structure.
Scope distinction: This section handles the sales-side organization of leads that are not ready to buy. The actual email campaigns, customer lifecycle communication, loyalty programmes, and retention activities are covered in Pillar 8.
## 12. Sales performance and lead conversion reporting
The business should be able to determine not just how many leads it receives, but how effectively it handles and converts them.

### Tasks and activities
- Define the sales and lead management KPIs relevant to the business.
- Configure reports for total leads received.
- Measure lead volume by source and campaign where data is available.
- Measure lead response times.
- Track contact rates.
- Track qualification rates.
- Measure the percentage of qualified leads progressing through each pipeline stage.
- Track quotation and proposal acceptance rates.
- Measure lead-to-customer conversion rates.
- Track sales cycle length.
- Monitor lost leads and reasons for lost opportunities.
- Measure revenue generated from converted leads.
- Compare conversion performance across lead sources.
- Evaluate sales performance by product, service, team, or salesperson where appropriate.
- Identify pipeline bottlenecks and stages where prospects frequently drop off.
- Build CRM dashboards and recurring reports.
- Establish a reporting schedule and assign responsibility for reviewing results.
### Key deliverables
- Lead management KPI framework.
- Sales performance dashboard.
- Lead source conversion reports.
- Pipeline conversion reports.
- Lead response-time reports.
- Sales cycle and win-rate reports.
- Lost-lead analysis.
- Revenue and sales performance reports.
- Recurring sales reporting procedure.
## 13. CRM integrations, workflow governance and data security
The CRM should work with the business's other relevant systems without creating fragmented records or unreliable information.

### Tasks and activities
- Identify systems that need to exchange information with the CRM.
- Connect the CRM to relevant platforms, such as:
  - Website and landing pages.
  - Advertising lead forms.
  - Email platforms.
  - Appointment scheduling tools.
  - E-commerce platforms.
  - Payment systems, where relevant.
  - Telephony and approved messaging tools.
  - Customer databases.
  - Analytics and reporting platforms.
- Define what information should pass between connected systems.
- Test that data is transferred correctly.
- Check for duplicate records and conflicting information.
- Establish which system is the primary source of truth for important records.
- Configure basic CRM notifications, reminders, and assignment rules.
- Define user roles and access permissions.
- Establish procedures for removing access when employees leave.
- Define data backup and recovery requirements.
- Establish data retention and deletion procedures.
- Review privacy, consent, and applicable data protection requirements.
- Document integrations and system dependencies.
### Key deliverables
- CRM integration map.
- Configured CRM integrations.
- Data exchange and field mapping documentation.
- System ownership and source-of-truth rules.
- User access and permission policy.
- CRM data security and governance procedures.
- Integration testing report.
Scope distinction: Basic CRM notifications, reminders, and lead assignment rules can be configured here. More advanced automation across several platforms, complex conditional workflows, and business-wide process orchestration belong to Pillar 10.
## 14. Sales process optimization and continuous improvement
Once the lead management and sales systems are operating, they should be reviewed regularly to identify weaknesses and improve conversion performance.

### Tasks and activities
- Review the performance of each sales pipeline stage.
- Identify stages where leads are frequently lost.
- Investigate slow response times and missed follow-ups.
- Review lead qualification criteria against actual customer outcomes.
- Identify lead sources producing poor-quality inquiries.
- Examine common objections and reasons for lost sales.
- Review sales scripts and enablement materials.
- Identify gaps in the information provided to prospects.
- Assess whether lead assignment and routing rules are effective.
- Review the accuracy and completeness of CRM records.
- Gather feedback from salespeople and relevant business personnel.
- Recommend improvements to sales processes and follow-up procedures.
- Update CRM configurations and documentation when necessary.
- Establish periodic reviews of the sales system.
### Key deliverables
- Sales process performance assessment.
- Lead conversion bottleneck report.
- Sales improvement recommendations.
- Updated sales playbook and procedures.
- CRM improvement recommendations.
- Periodic sales system review report.
- Continuous improvement plan.

What completing Pillar 7 should achieve
By the end of this pillar, the business should have:
- A centralized and organized system for receiving leads.
- A properly configured CRM or equivalent lead management system.
- A defined method for qualifying and prioritizing prospects.
- A clear sales pipeline showing where every active opportunity stands.
- Defined lead ownership, response times, and follow-up procedures.
- Sales materials that help the business communicate its value and handle objections.
- Organized appointment, quotation, and proposal processes where needed.
- Reports showing how leads progress into paying customers.
- A documented process for identifying and fixing sales conversion problems.
How Pillar 7 connects to the other pillars
Pillar	Primary responsibility
Pillar 6: Customer Acquisition	Attracts potential customers and generates leads.
Pillar 7: Lead Management & Sales Enablement	Organizes, qualifies, follows up with, and converts leads.
Pillar 8: Email Marketing, Retention & Customer Experience	Builds customer relationships, encourages repeat purchases, and improves retention.
Pillar 9: Analytics, Tracking & Attribution	Measures and connects performance data across the marketing and sales system.
Pillar 10: Marketing Automation, Integration & Optimization	Automates and coordinates processes across the connected systems.

The key distinction: Pillar 7 makes sure the business knows who its prospects are, what stage they are at, who is responsible for them, and what needs to happen next. Pillar 8 takes a broader view of the customer relationship, including what happens after the sale.
